## 1. Bottom line, and what to do first

1. **Nothing in the project has ever run the regime in which grokking is documented.** Every run streamed procedural data with a moving difficulty, used ≤ 10k optimizer updates, decayed only part of the weights, and selected checkpoints by an IID score. The standard recipe (a finite training set memorised first, a held-out split from the same rule, full or large batch, strong weight decay on all weights, constant or warmup-then-constant learning rate, ≥ 1e5 updates, no early stopping) was handed to the owner on December 17, 2025 by a Gemini Deep Research report, and never run. The repository cannot run it today: `--fixed_data` has no same-distribution held-out split, `mod_add` draws a new modulus per example, there is no preset below nano (41.6M parameters, 39 % of them the 32k-row embedding table), and there is no full-batch mode. This is draft [NF-13](audit-2026-10-27-drafts/) and avenue A1–A3.
2. **The early "grokking failed" conclusions were measured with a broken objective.** From the first commit until PR #80 (December 20) the labels were shifted twice, so the model was trained to predict the token two ahead. Until PR #110 (December 25) prompt tokens were in the loss, so the December 19 `mod_add` plateau at loss 0.533 is exactly the entropy of the random operands (2·ln 97 over 17.2 supervised tokens = 0.532). The three Grokfast runs of December 17–18 went NaN at micro-steps 350–400, 1350–1400 and 3050–3100; a simulation of the filter as coded (#137) predicts overflow at 383, 1357 and 3058. The chats blamed λ, warmup and weight decay 1.0, and weight decay 1.0 became the default on that advice. None of those runs says anything about grokking.
3. **The curriculum controller cannot reach its own gates.** Four mechanisms, all demonstrated on DAG-v12's `metrics.0.json`: the EMA decay is `1 − eval_interval/2000` (0.5 at the owner's interval, 0.0 at ≥ 2000); the train-eval set that feeds the gates is sampled by the static `TASK_BASE_WEIGHTS` table, so 68 % of it is locked tasks; the controller raises difficulty whenever the EMA passes 0.85 and the unlock gate needs four evaluations at ≥ 0.98, so copy toggled between difficulty 0.60 and 0.65 eleven times with accuracy swinging 0.97 ↔ 0.5; and locked tasks accumulate a chance-level EMA, so the one task that unlocked (parity) was demoted twice in its first three evaluations. The relaxed gates everyone believed were in place (PR #107, 0.70/0.50) were never merged; the band is hard-coded 0.85/0.55.
4. **Token accounting changed meaning on December 25 and nobody adjusted.** `tokens_seen`, `--alg_tokens`, the LR and difficulty progress, "Progress" and "Tok/s" count only supervised answer tokens since prompts were masked. DAG-v12's "100M tokens" was about 317M full-sequence tokens; its batch was 106–151 examples, not 96; and `--warmup_steps` is converted with a fixed 40 counted tokens per example when the real figure is 4.6–11, so warmup lasted 4–9× what was asked.
5. **The weight-norm story behind #186 is mostly wrong.** In `final_model.pt`, 31,879 of the 32,016 embedding rows belong to tokens the data never uses. Through the tied output head they all receive a softmax gradient every step; they drifted together to norm 1.08 (cosine 0.915 to one direction) by step 10k, carry 98.7 % of the embedding's squared norm, and were then frozen by Adam's epsilon. The *decayed* blocks grew more (129 → 307) than the exempt embedding (81 → 194). The logged weight norm is not a grokking signal, and exempting the embedding did not cause its growth.
6. **The eval harness is better than the previous review said, and still not trusted.** A perfect answerer scores 0.833 through `eval_hub` (not 0.779; the previous review's own table summed to 0.833), and 1.000 on all 31 conditions once the 32-token cap (#142) is honoured. The remaining measurement defects are in the drafts (grid version never bumped, `examples` are the first ten failures, eval batch ≈ 5, degenerate diagnostics, two seeds and batch sizes between `eval_hub` and the in-training eval).

**Order of work**, with the reasons in the sections below:

1. Fix the existing issue bodies that the audit found wrong (section 4, list (d) in the companion file): PR #107, the oracle number, the compare `=` rate, the #186 rationale, the PR attributions. Then post the drafts the owner accepts.
2. Step 0 of the new #182 fix order: make pytest collect (#166), land the CPU invariant suite (#184) with the oracle-through-every-path test, CI (#188).
3. Land the measurement fixes (#142, #143, #160, #164, #165, #178, #161) and the token-accounting fixes (drafts NF-09, NF-10, NF-50), so the next run can be read.
4. Build the finite-set path (draft NF-13: held-out split, fixed modulus, a sub-nano preset or a standalone harness, full-batch mode, `--decay_embeddings`) and run avenue A1: the standard recipe at classic scale, with a weight-decay sweep on all weights, three seeds. Minutes to hours per seed on the 4060. This is the first experiment that can answer "can this setup grok at all".
5. Only after a positive control: the curriculum-versus-balanced-sampling test at matched compute (A4), single-task learnability at nano (A5), and the small-vocabulary change (B1). Architecture comparisons (`shared_loop`, latent, ACT, SSM) stay last; #135 and #145 block them, and every report ranked them after a positive grokking control.

## 2. How the audit was done

**Agents.** 27 reading or probing agents ran in parallel from one common brief (repo read-only; CPU only; every claim cited to `file:line` at `774513d`, an artifact value, or a quoted transcript line; each claim marked Demonstrated or Plausible; verdicts in both directions). Scopes: `model.py`; `train.py` helpers; the training loop; every CLI flag and README command (run on CPU); `data.py` and `curriculum.py`; `eval_hub.py`, `utils.py`, `eval/`, `tests/`; PRs #1–#70 and #71–#199 (bodies, full diffs, reviews, Codex task files); all 64 issues with comments and the local drafts; Codex tasks 1–65 and 66–131; ChatGPT chats in three slices; Gemini chats in three; Claude chats in six; the 16 design reports; the surviving artifacts; the lexical-grokking worktree and notes; the previous review's working files. Three further agents merged the reports into the companion files, and four wrote the drafts.

**Verification.** Every candidate issue was checked at least twice: by the agent that found it (with a probe where cheap), by the merge pass (which recorded every disagreement between agents), and for the 18 claims most likely to become issues or to change an existing one, by the coordinator against the code or artifact directly (`VERIFIED_BY_COORDINATOR.md` in the audit bundle). Cross-agent agreement is recorded per finding: the 40-tokens-per-example warmup bug was found by 9 agents, pad = EOS by 6, the supervised-token budget by 6, the EMA decay formula by 6, the missing finite-set regime by 5. One agent's claim that the seven December 23 result files were produced inside the PR #83–#103 scoring bug window was checked and refuted (the files record commits that already contain the fix). One agent's claim that those files are lost was wrong (they are in `eval_results/`).

**Coverage, in numbers.** 12,589 lines of code and tests read line by line; 75 `train.py` and 19 `eval_hub.py` flags tabled with their read sites; 135 PRs; 64 issues and 14 comments; 131 Codex tasks (of which 94 ended "Testing: not run"); 48 + 18 + 16 chats (≈ 7.8M characters); 16 design reports; 3,476 rows of `metrics.0.json`; four checkpoints; seven result files. The 330 `file:line` citations in the posted issues were re-checked mechanically: all still hold.

**What the agents could not do.** No GPU, no `torch.compile`, no Mamba kernels, no language-phase data (offline). Two incidents limited the probes: a training-loop agent's DataLoader-worker probes pushed the machine to 0.4 GB free RAM and grew the pagefile into the disk (killed; from then on one small CPU process at a time, no workers), and the session's usage cap cut four agents mid-task (three were resumed; the fourth, the first half of a chat that turned out to be one log pasted four times, was dropped as redundant). The capability map of `best_model.pt` across tasks and lengths was skipped for memory.

## 6. Avenues

The companion file has 43 avenues with cost, dependencies and sources, plus 57 ideas from the chats that were proposed and dropped (each with its origin quote: supervised halting and deep supervision, a loop-count curriculum, per-iteration readout, StableMax, GrokTransfer, two-set training, learning-progress curriculum controllers, reversed-digit targets, a 16k or digit-only vocabulary, compute-matched `shared_loop`, an external reference model through the harness, and more). The ranked top 10:

1. **A1. The standard finite-set recipe at the classic scale** (`mod_add` with a fixed p, d 128 / 2 layers, digit vocabulary, weight decay on all weights, full batch, constant LR, 1e5 updates, three seeds): the only experiment that answers the question directly; minutes to hours per seed; 13 reports asked for it.
2. **A2. A same-distribution held-out split in the repo's data path**: without it A1 cannot be scored in the repo and every "IID" number stays a shifted-distribution number.
3. **A3. A weight-decay sweep on all weights, embeddings included, tied and untied**: the one knob the literature agrees on, never swept here.
4. **A4. Curriculum versus fixed balanced sampling at matched compute**: the direct test of the ignored warning.
5. **D1. Oracle and constant-output model through every eval path as the acceptance gate**: two minutes on CPU; the precondition for reading any number.
6. **A5. Single-task learnability at nano, fixed difficulty, no curriculum**: what the actual model learns in the actual budget, before variants are compared.
7. **B1. A small vocabulary for the algorithmic phase**: removes 39 % of nano's parameters from outside the decayed set and fixes #156 and #178 at once.
8. **D3 + D4. Budgets in optimizer updates; log the train/held-out gap, the used/unused norm split and measured parameter deltas**: without these a grokking run cannot be recognised.
9. **A6. Grokfast as a matched ablation after #137** (λ 2, α 0.98, accumulation 1): the only acceleration ever planned, never validly tested.
10. **A7. StableMax / softmax-collapse controls, fp32 versus bf16**: a cheap alternative account of why nothing grokked.

The project's own hypothesis (C1, a compute-matched `shared_loop` ladder) comes next, after a positive control; it is blocked on #145 and #135.
