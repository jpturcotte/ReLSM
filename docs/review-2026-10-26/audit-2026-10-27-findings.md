## 2. Per-row detail

**NF-01 — `local_window` NaN.** `_select_kv_source` takes `h[:, -cfg.local_window:, :]` and `_apply_thought_loop` slices the padding mask the same way; for any row whose last `local_window` positions are pad, every key is `-inf`, the hand-written softmax in `CrossAttention` returns NaN, and `z.mean()` broadcasts it into the row. code-model's probe: `latent`, `act`, `ssm` with `local_window` 2 and 4 on a batch of (10 real tokens; 5 real + 5 pad) give NaN logits and `loss=nan`; `ssm_mem` stays finite because the memory tokens are unmasked keys; without padding all are finite. Training collates with right padding to the longest row, so with `--local_window N` almost every real batch has such a row. No recorded run used the flag (grep of transcripts, `eval_results`, DAG-v12 summary); the Dec-31 chat reviewing PR #126 wrote "Handling of `local_window` for thought masks is correct". It matters because it is the one knob the design offers to make the thought read cheaper, and it cannot train. Fix: select the window per row from valid positions (gather on `attention_mask.cumsum`) or use SDPA's safe softmax; assert at least one unmasked key per row; fold into the #135 redesign. Severity: code-model says P1 conditional; by the brief's rule it breaks a conditional path (P2).

**NF-02 — embedding norm is an early, self-limiting dead-row drift; the decayed blocks grew more.** Mechanism (code-model CM-2): with the tied head every vocabulary row gets a gradient through the softmax denominator on every step; AdamW normalises the step so a tiny sign-consistent push moves a row by about `lr` per step, and `tok_emb` is exempt from decay (#186). Measured on DAG-v12 `final_model.pt` (step 77,503): 3,000 curriculum examples use 137 of 32,016 ids (artifacts: 135 from 2,000 examples); the 31,879 unused rows have mean norm 1.082 (std 0.008, init 0.453), cosine 0.915 to one common direction, and carry 98.7 % of the embedding's squared norm; the 137 used rows average 1.893 and are nearly orthogonal to it (0.107); along that direction a dead token's logit is about −20. Refinement (artifacts AR-5, from the Adam state in the same checkpoint): the unused rows' `exp_avg_sq` has median 1.7e-23 (mean 1.6e-22, max 4.1e-18) against 1.7e-8 for used rows, `|exp_avg|` 4.0e-11, update ratio 0.000; with `sqrt(v)` ≈ 4e-12 ≪ eps 1e-8 the per-element step is `lr·m/eps` ≈ 4e-3·lr, i.e. nothing. The logged `weight_norm.embedding` series confirms the timing: 81.0 (step 50), 175.2 (10k), 186.4 (20k), 190.4 (30k), 193.7 (50k), 194.45 (77.5k), so the drift happened in the first ~10k micro-steps (~830 updates) while the dead logits were still near zero, and once the common direction put them at ≈ −20 (p ≈ 2e-9) the gradient fell below eps and Adam froze them; between steps 68,000 and 77,503 the unused rows changed by 8.7e-4 relative, the used rows by 8.2e-2. CM-2's tiny-model probe ("no plateau" up to 1,500 steps, vocab 2,000) never reached that regime; lexical's nano check shows the same eps dominance from ~200 updates. The global norm (artifacts AR-6; tied tensor counted once, 358.07 = sqrt(407.46² − 194.45²)) went 152.6 → 358.1, and its non-embedding part 129.3 → 307.2 (50k) → 300.7: the 42 decayed tensors grew by 178, the 14 exempt ones (embedding + 13 RMSNorm) by 113, and the embedding part is flat after 20k. Weight decay 0.1 at peak lr 1.5e-3 removes 1.5e-4 per update; over 6,458 updates the cumulative shrink is exp(−0.1·Σlr) ≈ 0.53, so decay could at most halve a weight with no gradient and could not act during the ~800 updates in which the drift happened; the 2 % fall from 50k to 77.5k during LR decay is its only sign. The PR #196 toy overstates the speed (0.159 → 4.232 in 1,500 steps, cosine 0.999); at nano the rows grow 3.0× in 2,000 updates (2.3× with the table decayed) and decaying the table tightens the collapse direction (cosine 0.984 vs 0.898) rather than preventing it (lexical L2/L5); the dead rows' momentum points partly back along the common direction (cos −0.6), consistent with an overshoot that stopped. Why it matters: counter-review B7 and #186 read "the norm grew instead of shrinking because embeddings were exempt"; both parts grew, decay at this step count cannot produce a fall, the embedding part is a dead-row event that stops by itself, and the #186 rationale ("absent tokens get no gradient") has the sign wrong. Disagreement: code-model's "6,458 updates at `lr·wd` cannot counter a drift of `lr` per step" should read "decay cannot act during the ~800 updates in which the drift happens" (artifacts). Fix: log norms split into used rows / unused rows / decayed tensors; a vocabulary mask or compact vocabulary removes the early drift and 16.3M dead parameters (and changes nothing late in training); gate gradients of never-seen rows (lexical §5); a decayed embedding would, after the freeze, shrink the dead rows with nothing opposing it.

**NF-03 — pad == EOS.** `prepare_tokenizer` and `train.py:1954-1955` set `pad_token = eos_token` (Llemma has no pad token; both ids are 2). PR #95 appends EOS to every example and PR #126 derives the model's padding mask from `input_ids == pad_token_id`, so (a) the real EOS is masked as a key for every later position, (b) `_build_padding_mask`'s early exit never fires in training and the `is_causal=True` fast path is dead (every batch takes the dense-mask SDPA path, with a host-device sync), (c) in the thought loop and memory compressor the end-of-answer position is invisible to the latent variants, (d) `generate()`'s EOS fill for finished rows is masked on the next step. gemini-a's tiny-model probe: with an EOS at index 3 of 8, logits at positions 3–7 move by up to 0.046. Effect on current numbers: none (one example per row, EOS last, label dropped); code-model measured padding invariance to 1e-7. It becomes a defect for any packed or multi-document layout (the language phase) and it defeats the `with mask` / `without mask` comparison the Dec-31 chat wanted. Fix: a distinct pad id (reserved id or an added token with a resized embedding) or a mask built from the collator's lengths; a CPU test that the mask is True only at pad positions. Disagreement: gh-prs-b, review-files and codex-b want #172 rescoped to own it; code-model wants only an ADR line; claude-b2 adds that exact-length bucketing would remove the need for a mask at all.

**NF-04 — padding-only branch drops causality.** `Attention.forward` combines `mask + padding_mask` when both exist and falls back to `mask = padding_mask` alone when `mask is None`; the SDPA call then runs with `is_causal=False`, giving full bidirectional attention. The Dec-31 chat found this ("Lost Causal Masking When Only Padding Mask Present") and the fix built the causal mask upstream in `forward()`, but the fallback was kept. Both current callers pass both masks, so it is dead; any diagnostic, test or new variant that passes only a padding mask gets non-causal attention with no error. Fix: assert or build the causal mask inside `Attention.forward`; add a logits-equality check with a dummy padding mask to the #184 suite. Plausible (read only).

**NF-05 — `position_ids` past the chunk.** `Attention.forward` slices the RoPE cache to `T + past_len` and then indexes it with `position_ids`; a caller with per-row offsets (the usual reason to pass `position_ids`, e.g. left padding) gets `IndexError: index 6 is out of bounds for dimension 0 with size 6`. No current caller does this (`train.py:795` forwards a hook-captured `arange`). Fix: slice to `max(seq_len, position_ids.max()+1)` or drop the parameter.

**NF-06 — SSM runs lose block diagnostics.** `get_transformer_blocks` takes the `layers` attribute and keeps modules that have both `attn` and `ff`; `SSMBlock` has `ff` only, so the list is empty and `resolve_diagnostic_modules` returns only `['embedding', 'head']`. Per-block gradient/weight norms and attention probes are therefore absent for `ssm`/`ssm_mem` runs with no warning. Fix: match `SSMBlock` explicitly or filter on `ff` only.

**NF-07 — ACT/latent knobs not controllable.** `halt_threshold` is a dataclass default (0.99) with no CLI path; `K`, `min_K`, `max_K` are config fields read from the checkpoint and `eval_hub.py`/`utils.py` have no override (grep for `max_K`, `halt_threshold`: nothing), so the Dec-8 design's own validation ("allow the loop to run for up to 30 steps" on deeper inputs) cannot be run. The Dec-9 ladder made "histogram of halting steps by task type; correlation of steps with correctness" the Exp3 win condition; at HEAD only a window mean `train/act/inner_steps` is logged and `model.aux["inner_steps"]` is averaged and discarded. With #132 (no gradient to the halt head) that histogram would have shown the collapse in the first log. Fix: `--halt_threshold` (optionally annealed 0.7 → 0.99), `--eval_K`/`--eval_max_K` written into the eval JSON, a per-task histogram of `steps_used` and mean steps for correct vs incorrect at eval. Complements #132 (add there as acceptance items).

**NF-13 — no runnable finite-set grokking regime.** Grokking as reported (Power 2022, Nanda 2023) needs a finite table split into train and a disjoint same-distribution test. At HEAD: the fixed set is 5,000 draws with replacement from a space of 10⁵–10⁸ strings per task (4,860 distinct texts; mod_add has 479 distinct pairs under 300-odd moduli); the training-time "IID" eval uses final-phase params while the fixed set is the 0.5 phase, so the exact-text overlap is 0/200 for 8 of 10 tasks because the distributions differ, not because of a split (lexical probe; successor train digits 8–16 vs eval 20–32); for a single modulus the expected test-pair overlap would be 19 % and no `--mod` flag exists (claude-a2, claude-a1); the Dec-19 grid was two-thirds out-of-modulus. The smallest preset is nano (41.6M, 6 layers); the recommended d128/L2 body built on the 32k vocabulary is 4,620,928 parameters of which 4,096,000 are the tied embedding (88.6 %; 80.0 % at 16,384), so the body is 0.5M (gemini-a probe A). Full batch is impossible (token-budget batching capped at 4× examples), the default LR decays over the last 60 % (`wsd`), and no recorded run used constant LR, ≥ 1e5 steps or a held-out same-rule split (gemini-a). Why it matters: "every grokking attempt failed" was concluded without the regime ever being runnable. Fix: `FixedAlgorithmicDataset(split=…)` from one enumerated pool, a `--mod`/fixed-p option, a `grok` preset (1–2 layers, d128, no dropout), a digit-and-symbol tokenizer, a full-batch map-style loader, and `--lr_schedule constant` in a documented preset. Difficulty 0.0 is a real band (copy 1–3 items vs 3–6 at 0.2; mod_add modulus 17–50 vs 50–200). History agrees: run4 had five tasks hit 0.00 at step 8k and run5 at 23k, run6 with `--min_difficulty 0.2` pinned 8 of 10 tasks at 0.2 (chatgpt-b); the first DAG run drove locked tasks to 0.05 by step 8000 before PR #109 stopped stepping locked tasks (gh-prs-b). **NF-24 — no non-finite guard.** `GradScaler(enabled=(dtype == torch.float16))` is disabled under bf16 (chosen whenever the GPU supports it) and on CPU, so `scaler.step` is a plain `optimizer.step()` with no inf/NaN skip. Fix: if `grad_norm` is not finite, `zero_grad()`, skip the step, count and log it (also the "AMP skips" counter #148 asks for).

**NF-25 — hidden settings.** `build_optimizer` returns `AdamW(param_groups, lr=lr, betas=(0.9, 0.95))` with no flag and no entry in `hyperparameters`; the grokking recipes the owner emulated use (0.9, 0.98), and lexical notes a preprint linking β₂ 0.95 to rare-token logit drift. `cudnn.deterministic=True` and `benchmark=False` (PR #9, "reproducibility") run on every CUDA job, slow conv/SDPA kernels, and are not sufficient for determinism anyway (no `use_deterministic_algorithms`; DataLoader workers change the stream). **NF-29 — attention entropy over padding.** The probe batch is a right-padded `FixedAlgorithmicDataset` batch (38.5 % trailing padding at 48 tokens, 54.6 % at 128); `run_eval_diagnostics` samples 32 query positions per head uniformly over `B·T` and `compute_attention_probs` builds only a causal mask, so pad queries and pad/EOS keys are averaged in and the model's own `padding_mask` (NF-03) is ignored. **NF-30 — FF "sparsity".** `_make_ff_hook` captures the output of `block.ff` (the SwiGLU down-projection added to the residual) and counts `|x| < 1e-4`; that is not activation sparsity (the gated hidden units). **NF-31 — "weight entropy" is a histogram.** `compute_weight_entropy` bins `abs(param)` into 256 buckets and takes the Shannon entropy of the histogram, logged as `eval/weight/entropy/last_block` and `/head`. **NF-35 — deterministic carries.** `addition` picks digit bounds by bucket: (0, 4) for d < 0.3, (0, 9) for 0.3 ≤ d < 0.7, (5, 9) for d ≥ 0.7. With (5, 9) every column sum is ≥ 10 so every column carries; with (0, 4) none can. Probe (2,000 per difficulty): `all_columns_carry` 2,000/2,000 at d = 0.8, 0.95, 1.0; `any_carry` 0/2,000 at 0.05 and 0.2; columns whose carry-out depends on the carry-in (digit sum 9) appear in 0 examples outside the medium bucket and 22 % inside. So the final phase ("3–6 digits, high carry pressure") needs only "add 1 to every column but the last, prepend 1"; the 20 % easy mix is carry-free; the IID grid at 0.5 (bounds 0–9) is where carry is a real decision, so the addition IID/training gap (#155, #162) is a different algorithm, not only a digit histogram. Coordinator confirmed the bounds. Fix: sample bounds independently of difficulty or make carry probability an explicit, reported parameter.

**NF-36 — equal operand lengths.** One `digits` value is drawn and both operands get exactly that many digits in `addition`, `multiplication` and `comparison` (`same_len` 2,000/2,000 at all eight difficulties). Compare can never be solved by length and never tests it; a one-digit lookup gives 89 %; addition never shows ragged operands so alignment is never learned; multiplication never mixes 1- and 3-digit operands. `utils.build_dataset` passes the same parameters so IID and OOD inherit the restriction. Fix: draw the two lengths independently within the phase range; add a `length_mismatch_frac` statistic; a mixed-length OOD axis is free.

**NF-37 — successor is a copy task.** `successor` samples `n = randint(0, 10**digits − 1)`, so the trailing run of 9s has length k with probability 10⁻ᵏ: no trailing 9 in 89.3–90.9 % of examples at every difficulty, at most one in 98.8–99.3 %, three or more in 0.00–0.25 %. The comment at `data.py:58` says the ramp to 32 digits exists "to force long-range carry propagation"; it never does. What the ramp produces is a 26-token (p50) verbatim copy of the prompt digits plus one increment, which is exactly what overflows `alg_seq_len=48` (1,944/2,000 examples longer than 48 tokens at d = 0.95; #139), so raising the length for successor buys nothing. `chain` is the task that actually samples its carry run. Fix: sample the trailing-9 run explicitly, or cap successor at the digit count the carry structure needs; rewrite the comment.

**NF-38 — memorisable easy fifth.** Within 2,000 generated examples at d = 0.05: parity 88.2 % duplicate texts (178 distinct cores; only 62 bit strings of length 2–5 exist), dyck 70.1 %, addition 58.4 %, chain 57.5 %, copy/reverse 50.1 %, multiplication 38–40 % for d ≤ 0.35, compare 35.1 %, successor 39.7 %. `sample_difficulty_smooth`, `sample_difficulty_warmup_ramp` and `CurriculumDifficultySampler` all draw `uniform(0, 0.3)` with probability `easy_mix_frac` = 0.2 for the whole run, so in a 4,000-example pass 3.4 % repeat an earlier example (multiplication 8.1 %), and over 100,000 examples per epoch and ~92 replays (#140) the easy fifth is a few hundred strings per task seen thousands of times. The "infinite data" framing (#140, #169) is wrong twice over, and #140's acceptance test ("epochs share no examples") cannot pass for parity/dyck/compare at easy phases without changing the tables. Fix: report distinct-example counts per task and phase at startup; draw the easy mix from a phase with a large support (parity length ≥ 6) or dedupe within an epoch.

**NF-39 — the smooth staircase.** The sigmoid width is `transition_width / 4` = 0.0125, so the deterministic value jumps 0.28 → 0.42 between progress 0.13 and 0.17, is 0.95 at 0.62 and 1.00 from 0.8; with noise 0.05 and clipping, 40 % of draws at progress ≥ 0.7 are exactly 1.0. The four plateaus 0.25/0.45/0.70/1.0 hit the 4–7-phase tables so that the middle phases (copy (4,8) and (6,10), addition (1,4)) occur 0–2 % of the time; the last 38 % of the budget trains at the final phase plus easy mix. A design mismatch rather than a bug; it changes how "curriculum" should be read for non-task-curriculum runs (and with workers the schedule never leaves plateau one, #141).

**NF-40 — worker seed collisions.** `AlgorithmicDataset.__iter__` seeds `random.Random(self.seed + worker_id)`, so `--seed 43` with workers gives its worker 0 the stream `--seed 42` gave its worker 1; two "different seed" runs with 4 workers share a quarter of their data. **NF-41 — grid version and grid coupling.** `git log -S OOD_GRID_VERSION` shows the string written once in `7cfedae` (2025-12-12, a date before the repository existed) and never changed, while the grid changed in `e5905f0` (12-19), `37851cb` (12-20), PR #82, #113 and #114; `grep '"grid_version"' eval_results/*.json` gives 14 hits, all `2024-09-05`, so the one field meant to tell grids apart cannot. **NF-42 — generation batch ≈ 5.** `evaluate_condition` slices the dataset into chunks of `batch_size`, `generate_batch` buckets each chunk by exact token length and then again by `batch_size`; with one length per prompt template a chunk of 32 splits into 4–10 micro-batches. **NF-43 — degenerate diagnostics.** Measured on 4,100 oracle prompts: `colon` 4,100, `answer` 4,100, `arrow` 200 (= all chain prompts, `->` is the operator), `fatarrow` 0, `equals` 112 (the 56 % of compare prompts with the "(>, <, or =)" hint); every generator ends in `Answer:`, so the cohorts split nothing and `--debug_eval_format_stats` is dead weight since PR #103/#116. **NF-44 — eval_hub vs in-training IID.** `eval_hub.py` defaults to `--seed 1234` and `--batch_size 32`; `evaluate_algorithmic` in `train.py` uses `seed=args.seed` (42) and a hard-coded `batch_size=8`; with per-condition seeds derived from the seed, the two paths draw different example sets, so README post-hoc numbers and the in-training curve are not on the same examples even before #160's per-process salting. **NF-45 — latent hazards.** (a) `utils.py:971-973` reads `params.get("valid_ratio", 0.5)` but `kwargs = dict(params)` keeps the key and `dyck_language` rejects it: `build_dataset("dyck", 2, {"max_depth": 5, "valid_ratio": 0.5}, 1)` raises `TypeError`; no current condition sets it. **NF-46 — tokenizer premise.** The Dec-17 report ranked "Llama 2 / BPE 32k" as "Insuffisant pour l'objectif Maths" (digits "Incohérent (agglomération)") and Llemma as "Le candidat le plus fort" because it supposedly re-trained the tokenizer to isolate digits; commit `e9de18b` landed the same day. **NF-47 — the train-eval set is not the training distribution.** The train-eval loader is an `AlgorithmicDataset` built with `difficulty_fn=eval_difficulty_fn` but no `weighting_fn` and no `weighting_adjust_fn`, so `_sample_task` falls to the `task_weighting == "adaptive"` branch and samples by `TASK_BASE_WEIGHTS` (multiplication 2.5, addition 2.0, reverse 1.5, copy/parity/successor/mod_add 1.0, chain 0.8, dyck/compare 0.7, the last three halved after 50 %); the DAG gates and curriculum weights are not consulted. Last-eval counts in DAG-v12: copy 64, reverse 83, compare 35, successor 68, parity 74, chain 58, addition 126, mod_add 80, multiplication 148, dyck 64 (sum 800); the six locked tasks are 544/800 = 68 % of the set against 1.95 % of training examples; the predicted shares (multiplication 22.5 %, addition 18.0 %) match the counts (18.5 %, 15.8 %). **NF-48 — locked tasks are tracked at chance and demoted on unlock.** `task_curriculum.update_metrics` runs for every task in `per_task_accuracy`, gated or not, so a locked binary task carries an EMA near 0.5 and a growing `step_count`. When the gate opens, `should_step_curriculum` waits only `unlock_warmup_evals` = `curriculum_min_task_evals` (2 in DAG-v12), then `step_curriculum` sees `step_count ≥ min_task_evals` and an EMA below 0.55 and subtracts 0.05. Parity: gate 0 → 1/3 at step 10,000, 2/3 at 11,000, 1.0 at 12,000; EMA while locked 0.47–0.58 (raw 0.43–0.64, chance for a 0/1 label); difficulty 0.20 (1,000–11,000) → 0.15 at 12,000 → 0.10 at 13,000 → 0.15 at 19,000 → 0.20 at 38,000 → … → 0.90 at 77,000, i.e. 26,000 micro-steps below its starting difficulty and the final-phase range (0.60+) only at 55,000; its IID score rose 0.55 → 0.94 as its difficulty reached the grid's range (the counter-review's "staircase"). code-train-b's tiny DAG probe shows the same rule on an untrained model (roots 0.20 → 0.00 in four evals). Every future unlock starts the same way for any task whose chance accuracy is below 0.55 (all but compare and dyck). Not in #157 or #141. Fix: on unlock reset `ema_acc`, `step_count` and `last_update_step` (or skip `update_metrics` while `gate == 0`) and start the controller after `unlock_warmup_evals` real evals; never lower a task below `init_difficulty` in its first N evals.

**NF-49 — the controller limit cycle.** With step 0.05, band 0.55–0.85, EMA decay 0.5 (NF-15) and a cooldown (500) shorter than the eval interval (inert), a task whose accuracy is ~1.0 at difficulty d and ~0.5 at d + 0.05 cannot settle: at d the EMA climbs above 0.85 in two evals (bump), at d + 0.05 it falls below 0.55 in two or three (demote). Copy in DAG-v12: 0.65 at 12k, 0.60 at 18k, 0.65 at 20k, 0.60 at 22k, 0.65 at 24k, 0.60 at 27k, 0.65 at 29k, 0.60 at 32k, 0.65 at 34k, 0.60 at 37k, 0.65 at 39k, 0.60 at 43k, 0.65 at 45k, 0.60 at 50k, 0.65 at 52k (eleven toggles); raw train-eval accuracy at 0.60: 0.96–0.99, at 0.65: 0.44–0.59; EMA peaks 0.87–0.88 and troughs 0.53–0.58; from 56,000 the raw score is exactly 0.609 (39/64) at every eval and the cycle stops because 0.609 sits inside the band. Reverse never cycled (0.61–0.68 at 0.65, inside the band from 13,000). The three training-loss spikes (steps 3,000, 6,050, 10,050: 0.24, 0.49, 0.50 against 0.03–0.08) each follow a bump. code-data's simulation with the copy/reverse cliff reproduces it (copy and reverse flip direction 99 times each between 0.60 and 0.65, EMA pinned at 0.597, nothing ever unlocks). Why it matters: the successor gate needs copy EMA ≥ 0.98 for 4 consecutive evals; in the cycle the EMA touches 0.87–0.88 for one eval and is pushed back, so even a perfect 10-item copier could not open it; this is the dynamic form of #157's "gates above the band". Fix (with #157): hysteresis with a smaller step near the top, decide difficulty on per-length accuracy rather than the mean, gate on accuracy at a fixed length rather than at the moving difficulty.

**NF-50 — the effective batch.** `AlgorithmicDataset` yields a batch when a length bucket holds `base_batch_size × 4` examples or when `count × max_len_in_bucket ≥ base_batch_size × max_seq_len` (96 × 48 = 4,608 tokens), with three buckets (≤ 16, 17–32, > 32 tokens): short tasks fill bucket 1 at up to 288 examples, 10–12-item copy/reverse fill bucket 3 at 96–135. Fix: log `len(batch["task"])` and the bucket id per micro-step (free); separate the truncation limit, the batch token budget and the bucket boundaries (#139 comment 2); or a fixed example count with padding; rename the flag.

**NF-51 — stored eval examples are failures.** `utils.py:1238-1248` adds an example to `collected_examples` only when `exact_match != 1.0`, up to 10. In the seven Dec-23 files `successor` has 0 examples in the four 5steps/20steps files (accuracy 1.0) and 10 in the other three; every task with accuracy < 0.95 has exactly 10; the 5 `sampled_examples` per condition are the unbiased ones.
