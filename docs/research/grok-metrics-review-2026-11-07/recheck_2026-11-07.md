# Recheck of `analysis.md` (2026-11-07)

**What this is.** An independent recheck of `analysis.md` in this folder: every number in sections 1, 2, 4 and 7 and in both tables, every literature claim, and a sweep of the 2025–2026 literature. I recomputed everything from the raw run files with my own code (not `curve_metrics.py`), using stdlib and numpy only. No torch, no checkpoints. I did not open `score.json` or `blind_review_2026-11-07/`. Step log: `recheck_progress.md`.

**Red-teamed.** Two independent red teams attacked this recheck and `analysis.md`: a Fable agent with full numerical access, and Codex run under the memory guard, whose sandbox blocked Python, so it checked code, sources and hand arithmetic. Every finding below that changes a conclusion was verified again against the data or source before it went in. Section 8 lists every red-team finding and what was done with it. Where they showed this recheck was wrong, the text now says so.

**Labels.** **Confirmed**: my recomputation or the source gives the same value. **Corrected**: the value or wording is wrong; the right one is given. **Unverifiable**: I could not check it with the allowed data. **[new test]**: a test this recheck ran that `analysis.md` did not.

## Conclusions first

1. **The analysis holds up.** Section 4 below has 81 audit rows, most covering several numbers. 12 rows have a correction, 2 are unverifiable and the rest are confirmed. Two more corrections in my first version were withdrawn after the red teams. Section 5 checks 15 literature claims and corrects 3. Most corrections are small range edits. Four points change a sentence's meaning, and one earlier claim of mine is withdrawn:
   - **gcd, plain accuracy, D above TA "in 3 of 3 seeds": wrong.** At the final eval it is 2 of 3. In seed 1, TA is 0.9800 and D 0.9776. It is 3 of 3 only on the late median, which the cited ranges don't use. The balanced-accuracy reversal (TA above D in 3/3) stands, so the conclusion survives with "2 of 3".
   - **"Held-out loss never rises above its starting value" in the 19 "no memorisation" runs: wrong for 6 of them.** TA_div and TA_sub peak at 1.04–1.08× their start. It is true for 13 of the 19.
   - **Held-out loss and accuracy are scored on different sets** (found by Codex, verified). `test_loss_unseen` is computed on every unseen pair, diagonals included (`grok_lab.py` line 534, mask `~seen`). The accuracy curve is off-diagonal only. So every loss-against-accuracy comparison in both documents mixes two sets: the loss-peak lead, the loss hump, and "held-out loss moves before accuracy". That includes the claim that every number uses unseen off-diagonal data. The comparisons are still descriptive, but they need relabelling, and loss logged on the accuracy mask (a9).
   - **t50's low spread rests on cells at the eval floor.** Its pooled log-sd of 0.133 includes 3 cells with zero spread (D_add, D_add2b, TA_sq) and 2 near the floor (D_sub, TA_div). Fable's red team corrected my first wording of "5 zero-spread cells". Without those 5 cells t50 is 0.173, against 0.155 for the first 0.99 crossing and 0.139 for t90. A rule that ignores t50 gives the same direction: dropping every cell where any measure has zero spread leaves t50 at 0.188 and the first crossing at 0.141. This is a sensitivity analysis, not proof that another timing has more power. Sustained-0.99 is worst under every rule (0.26–0.35); 1 − AUC is lowest (0.078–0.092). [new test]
   - **Withdrawn.** An earlier version of this recheck said "extrapolation is biased optimistic holds for D_mul only", from a hindcast scored against the first single eval at 0.99. Fable's red team showed that against the sustained crossing or the fitted trend, all six hindcastable runs (D_mul and D_sq, three seeds each) were optimistic. D_sq_s2's 21.6k projection, made at 10–12k, had already failed by 25k. The analysis's "biased optimistic" stands, with one refinement from Codex: the 15k D_mul forecasts of 27.5k and 28.3k (s0, s1) lie beyond the 25k horizon, so they are censored, not shown wrong. Forecasts made at 8k–12k (22k–25k) were falsified in all three seeds, and s2's 15k forecast (24.0k) too.
2. **The main conclusions follow from the numbers:**
   - the 0.99 rule measures "generalised by the horizon";
   - its states lump unlike runs together;
   - balanced accuracy is needed for gcd;
   - held-out loss and margins belong next to accuracy;
   - D_mul is the clearest delayed-generalisation run.

   Two of the analysis's own proposals are weaker than stated:
   - **The delay ratio is censored when Tmem is a few evals.** In TA runs Tmem is 200 updates, two evals. If evals were every 500 updates, TA_mul's ratio would read 2.0 unsmoothed; the 1.0 values in my first version also came from keeping a 5-eval median, which spans 2,500 updates at that spacing (both red teams). The flip comes from Tmem rounding up from 200 to 500. D_div and D_sq are stable. What spacing the project actually uses: the harness and train.py's table scorer evaluate every 100 updates (`--mod_table_eval_every`). train.py's LM eval runs every 500 micro-batches, which is 125 optimizer updates at 4× accumulation (Codex). [new test]
   - **The loss-peak sign fires on flat runs too.** A peak followed by a fall shows in 5 of 6 flat D runs; D_sqab_s1's highest loss is at the final eval. The sign separates TA_cubeab from D_cubeab only with a size condition: TA fell 9.5–17.7 % below its peak, D 0.7–0.9 %. It does not separate TA_sqab (2–4 %) from D_sqab (1–2 %). The loss here is the mixed-set loss above. [new test]
3. **What held up beyond its own claims.** The "3 se" rising screen survives a lag-1 AR(1) inflation of the standard errors: every "rising" call stays above 3 se, the lowest being D_sq_s1 at 3.3, a run already "converged" [new test]. This is a heuristic correction, not a HAC or block-bootstrap estimate (Codex), so call the screen "robust to a simple autocorrelation check", not settled. The β numbers, the D_mul prediction and the E1 out-of-sample check all reproduce exactly; Fable recomputed them independently.
4. **Literature.** All six claims marked "from memory" are right in substance. Each was checked against the full PDF.
   - **Corrected:**
     - Feldman–Zhang trained 2,000–4,000 models on 70 % subsets, not "about 10³";
     - Varma's "ungrokking" comes from further training on a subset smaller than the critical size, not from a run losing accuracy on its own;
     - Furman and Lau 2024 (arXiv 2402.03698) is withdrawn and merged into Lau et al.;
     - Guo states the strong binning sensitivity for MCE, not ECE.
   - **Opinion, not a source claim:** "CKA is poor at saying why" is the analysis's own view.
   - **Wider than the source:** Schaeffer et al. is about model scale, so applying it to training time extends it.
   - **A blog post:** the lit report's support for "loss moves before accuracy" ([20]) is a CSDN blog post. The project's own data (18/18) now carry that claim.
5. **New literature (2025 – Nov 2026).** About 30 primary sources; see section 6. Most of it supports the analysis's direction: threshold-free, level-and-shape summaries, held-out margin and loss next to exact match, and LLC left for later. Three findings change recommendations:
   - **Grokking outcomes depend on the numerical environment** (Ootani 2026, arXiv 2607.05104). Changing the CPU thread count, or CPU against GPU, "flip[s] a minority of same-seed outcomes" with no detectable change in the rate. Within one environment, this project has measured that same-seed CUDA reruns are bit-identical: 47 pairs, design doc line 318, plus E1 add_s0 against its rerun, identical in 251/251 rows. So phase 1's same-seed pairs are exact here, but results transfer across machines or thread counts only as seed rates. An earlier version of this recheck said the pairs were inexact; both red teams showed that was wrong, and it is withdrawn.
   - **A concrete resume-equivalence spec now exists** (NVIDIA Megatron Core blog, Oct 2026). Fingerprint the loss and the gradient norm every step, find the first step where they differ, and after a restore compare parameters, optimizer state, RNG state and data position. The post warns that printed loss values "are not sensitive enough".
   - **Don't read a representation metric at the transition as a converged value; flag censored runs** (Truong 2026, arXiv 2607.06639). It confirms the 3–5× figure for an MLP and gives 1.3–1.5× for a transformer. It applies to the squared-singular-value variant of effective rank (Codex). Trajectories through the transition are still informative.

   LLC stays at "not now", the analysis's verdict. Cullen et al. 2026 (arXiv 2603.01192) report estimates shifting by orders of magnitude with SGLD settings, but also find LLC trajectories informative at fixed settings. An earlier version of this recheck said "retire"; both red teams objected, and that is withdrawn.
6. **Two project facts the analysis missed:**
   - **train.py already logs per-module weight norms and grad/weight ratios**: `train/weight/norm/{key}` and `train/grad/ratio/{key}` for the embedding, head, block0, block_mid and block_last. Recommendation 6 is partly done. What's missing is per-MLP (not per-block) norms, a dormant fraction, and the measured update ratio (#149).
   - **Phase 1 does log Fourier and log-Fourier top-5 shares, but only for the TA arm.** Digit runs have no number embedding. Where present, the share does not lead accuracy consistently: it leads in TA_mul_s0 and TA_add_s0 and lags in TA_mul_s1 and TA_add2b_s0. It cannot be the cross-arm progress measure. [new test]

### 4.6 Section 7 (the proposed state rule): logic

- **The order of checks is sound.** It runs converged, rising, near-line, flat, stalled, and reproduces every state in `curve_results.json`.
- **"Flat at chance" uses kappa, which isn't per-eval.** The code doesn't use it either. See a4.
- **"Rising" is computed on raw accuracy.** A run whose accuracy climbs to the majority baseline (TA_sqab_s2) reads "rising". I tried β on chance-corrected accuracy, but it is unstable near zero (logit of clipped values), so per-eval kappa is the better fix.
- **The width cut-off of 0.35 sits inside quantisation.** TA_sub_s2 reads "gradual" at 0.37, which is t10 = 300 against t90 = 700, two eval steps apart. A width of 0 reads "gradual" because of a code bug.
- **The delay cut-off of 2 flips with eval spacing** (section 2), and on E1 (mul_s0 at 2.0 = 400 / 200).

## 7. What is settled and what is not

Settled (by data here, rechecked, and red-teamed):
- **The 0.99 first-crossing rule does not measure grokking.** 19 of the 33 runs it scores "groks" show no memorisation phase by the loss hump (1.00 in 13, 1.04–1.08 in 6). In 11 of them the delay ratio is censored.
- **gcd needs balanced accuracy.**
- **Sustained 0.99 has the worst seed spread here.** This holds under every floor-exclusion rule.
- **The β trend separates "still rising" from "flat".** This survives a simple autocorrelation check; a HAC or bootstrap check is still to do.
- **D_mul is the clearest delayed-generalisation run.**
- **β-line crossing forecasts run optimistic** against the sustained crossing (6 of 6 hindcastable runs).

Not settled:
- **Every cut-off of the new readout.** They are post hoc and quantisation-sensitive, and E1 has no hard cases.
- **Whether the loss peak predicts an accuracy onset.** It needs a drop-size rule and a held-out study.
- **D_mul at 100k.** The two fits now disagree even inside the power-law family (late-window s2 points to about 71k).
- **gcd's time course.** Balanced accuracy isn't logged per eval.
- **Whether any of this transfers to train.py.** Its table scorer evaluates every 100 updates. Its LM eval runs every 500 micro-batches, which is 125 optimizer updates. At coarse spacing both the delay ratio and the loss hump lose the fast runs.
- **Every loss-against-accuracy result.** Until loss is logged on the accuracy mask, these compare two different sets.
- **Delay ratio 2 as the definition of grokking.** By the delays Power et al. report, which are orders of magnitude, only D_mul (about 12) clearly qualifies in phase 1. Runs at 3–6 are mildly delayed.
